# 06 · Ink wash

Thin pigment washes with soft blooms and a few dark ink accents.
This differs from opaque Painted shapes and the powdery surface of Soft pastel.

Read the [shared prompt](../../references/prompts.md). Add the actual scene and palette, then this style block.

## Style prompt

```text
Interpret the source as a small, loose ink-wash illustration.
Use one primary ink, a softer related pigment, and a small earthy accent when needed.
Build the important forms with a few thin washes.

Show natural pigment blooms, soft spreading edges, and occasional darker pooling along a drying edge.
Add only a few firm brush accents to keep the main silhouette recognizable.
Let washes overlap locally, not across the whole canvas.

Keep the light areas and the spaces between forms empty.
Do not paint a full sky or cover open water with a background wash.
Avoid precise outlining, dense detail, and uniformly smooth digital gradients.

The wash should appear to sit inside the subject's marks.
Do not add a tinted paper sheet, a rectangular wash field, or texture outside the artwork.
```

## Preparation and checks

- Start with cutoff `0.065`; reduce it only if important pale wash edges disappear.
- Use pigment pairs for primary, accent, and soft tones when present.
- Preserve partial transparency inside the washes. Do not turn all pigment into opaque flat color.
- Check pale edges on the page background. Background removal must not create a rectangular haze.
