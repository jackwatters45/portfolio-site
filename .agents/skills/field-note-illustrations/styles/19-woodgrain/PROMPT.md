# 19 · Woodgrain

Flowing woodgrain lines sit inside printed forms.
This has more directional texture than Carved, but no physical wooden backing panel.

Read the [shared prompt](../../references/prompts.md). Add the actual scene and palette, then this style block.

## Style prompt

```text
Make a woodblock-style interpretation of the reference with visible grain within the printed shapes.
Use two or three restrained inks from the supplied palette.
Preserve the main silhouettes, perspective, and spaces between important forms.

Let long, slightly irregular grain lines flow along slopes and other surface directions.
Include only a few small knot-like turns in the grain.
Break some lines with rough cuts, uneven ink coverage, and worn print edges.

Use broad printed shapes without closing every open area.
Keep light spaces unprinted and let the grain reveal transparent channels within the pigment.
Do not spread grain across the empty canvas.

Avoid a photographed wooden board, plank edges, polished knots, raised relief, and cast shadows.
The subject should look printed with woodgrain, not built from glossy pieces of timber.
```

## Preparation and checks

- Start with cutoff `0.065`. Map each used pigment separately.
- Keep grain channels transparent and distinct from colored surface marks.
- At page size, the grain must remain directional rather than becoming dense visual noise.
- Keep the subject more important than knots. Regenerate if knots become large decorative objects.
