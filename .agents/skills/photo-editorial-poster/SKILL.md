# Photo Editorial Poster

Create one independent, high-end editorial poster for each uploaded photo. Never combine photos into a collage.

## Workflow

1. Inspect each source photo. Identify its subject, important relationships, color palette, and suitable framing.
2. If no photo is supplied, ask for an upload. Do not substitute an unrelated image.
3. Use an available image editing or generation tool that accepts the source image as a reference. Read its tool instructions first.
4. Preserve the source photograph in the top region. Prefer compositing the actual photograph with a separately generated illustration.
5. Generate and export each poster separately. Keep each source paired with its own output.
6. Inspect the finished image against the checks below. Correct defects before delivery.
7. Deliver the image files, not only a prompt. For Telegram requests, attach the files with the available attachment tool.

If image tools are unavailable, explain the limit. Provide a ready-to-use prompt if helpful, but do not claim an image was generated.

## Overall format

- Strict 3:4 vertical canvas. A suitable default is 1536 × 2048 pixels.
- Divide the canvas horizontally into two exactly equal sections.
- The photograph occupies the top 50%. The paper illustration occupies the bottom 50%.
- Make both sections feel like one refined art publication cover.
- Keep these dimensions exact through final compositing, rather than relying only on a model's layout approximation.

## Top half: original photograph

Preserve the original photo as faithfully as possible:

- Main composition, subjects, identity, facial features, and body proportions.
- Poses, expressions, clothing, objects, and spatial relationships.
- Realistic photographic texture, natural lighting, shadows, atmosphere, and original color mood.

Apply only subtle editorial color grading. Aim for a premium magazine photograph, contemporary art book, or independent publication.

Keep the image photorealistic and authentic. Avoid excessive retouching or artificial styling.

If the layout requires more background, extend sky, ground, walls, or the surrounding environment seamlessly. Never stretch, distort, reshape, replace, or alter the main subject. Do not sacrifice subject fidelity to fill the frame.

## Bottom half: minimal hand-drawn paper illustration

Extract the most recognizable visual elements. Preserve:

- The recognizable subject and essential silhouette.
- Essential proportions and key pose or gesture.
- Important objects and the core relationship between people and objects.

Remove unnecessary detail. Keep only enough information for immediate recognition.

Use delicate, slightly imperfect hand-drawn lines and a few bold acrylic-style flat color shapes. Include rough paper texture, handmade brush marks, organic edges, and subtle imperfections.

Keep the illustrated subject small and centered. It should occupy approximately 10–20% of the bottom half, with generous negative space.

Use rough white, warm off-white, or pale natural paper as the background. It should resemble minimal editorial book-cover stock.

Suggest the environment with only a few lines or small color shapes. Do not turn the lower section into a detailed painting.

## Color palette

Extract dominant colors from the original photo. Use no more than four main illustration colors.

Keep the palette restrained, harmonious, and sophisticated. Use bold but controlled flat color blocks. Preserve paper grain and handmade brush texture.

The illustration should read as a simplified color interpretation of the photograph. The four-color limit applies to the illustration, not the original photograph.

## Typography

Text is optional. Do not force it into the design.

Suitable elements include a short title, keyword, object name, location, year, number, or short phrase.

Use minimal, understated editorial typography. Let it interact naturally with the negative space and small illustration. Refer to art books, independent publishing, contemporary editorial design, and thoughtful children's picture books.

Use accurate spelling. Do not invent a location, date, or personal detail. Use supplied facts, ask when needed, or omit uncertain text.

## Visual character

Quiet, poetic, refined, minimal, innocent, relaxed, artistic, thoughtful, recognizable, and premium.

## Final checks

- One separate output per input photo; no multi-photo combinations.
- Exact 3:4 canvas and 50/50 horizontal split.
- Original subject identity and spatial relationships remain unchanged.
- Top region remains photographic, with only restrained grading.
- Bottom illustration remains small, recognizable, centered, and handmade.
- Paper texture remains subtle, with ample negative space.
- No more than four main illustration colors.
- Any text is minimal, accurate, and clearly rendered.
