# Rubber Stamp Travel Field Notes

Create one independent poster for each uploaded photo. Output each separately. Never combine multiple photos or make a collage.

The photo records the scene. A small, slightly ink-deficient rubber stamp captures its most memorable outlines.

## Workflow

1. Inspect each source photo. Identify its distinctive forms, scene relationships, and dominant colors.
2. If no photo is supplied, ask for an upload. Do not substitute an unrelated image.
3. Use an available image editing or generation tool that accepts the source photo as a reference. Read its instructions first.
4. Prefer compositing the actual source photograph beside a separately generated paper-and-stamp region. Do not regenerate the photograph.
5. Establish accurate location and year text from user input or reliable supplied metadata. Do not guess from appearance alone.
6. Generate, inspect, and export each poster separately. Keep each source paired with its own output.
7. Deliver image files, not only prompts. For Telegram requests, attach files with the available attachment tool.

If image tools are unavailable, explain the limit. Offer a ready-to-use prompt if useful, but do not claim an image was generated.

## Overall format

- Strict 4:3 landscape canvas. A suitable default is 2048 × 1536 pixels.
- Divide it into left and right regions without an obvious dividing line.
- Left: approximately 58% of the width, containing the original photo.
- Right: approximately 42% of the width, containing warm off-white aged paper.
- Keep these proportions through final compositing rather than relying only on model layout accuracy.

## Left region: original photograph

Faithfully preserve subject identity, terrain, architecture, plants, people, spatial relationships, lighting, shadows, authentic textures, and original color atmosphere.

Apply only restrained art-publication color grading and extremely subtle, fine-grained film noise.

Natural cropping is allowed for layout adaptation. Never stretch, distort, shift, replace, redraw, or otherwise alter the main subject. Do not remove people or objects from the photograph merely because they are omitted from the stamp.

## Right region: aged paper

Use warm off-white aged paper with subtle fibers, natural grain, light usage marks, and a matte surface.

Preserve large areas of unprinted paper. Whitespace is an essential part of the composition, not space to fill with decoration.

## Stamp content and scale

Extract the most location-distinctive outlines, architectural structures, terrain contours, plant forms, roads, shorelines, or other key relationships.

Compress these into a small multicolor rubber stamp. Do not replicate every element.

Keep only the information needed to recognize the location, subject, and scene relationships. Remove crowds, vehicles, dense windows, repetitive buildings, fragmented vegetation, decorative elements, and irrelevant backgrounds from the stamp.

Place the stamp in the lower-middle of the paper region. Its height should be approximately 30–38% of the right region's height. Leave ample whitespace around it.

Do not enlarge it into a standard illustration, full landscape painting, or brand logo.

### Organize the stamp from the source composition

- Iconic architecture: retain distinctive contours, roofs, domes, arches, towers, or main structures.
- Mountain settlements: compress buildings into a few terraced color blocks aligned with the terrain.
- Coastal scenery: retain mountain contours, settlement layers, shorelines, and sparse intermittent water ripples.
- City panoramas: retain the main skyline, one iconic building, and one or two distant mountain layers, when present.
- Natural landscapes: retain primary mountains, trees, shorelines, or road orientations.
- Foreground occlusions: retain their outlines when important to the original scene's narrative.

Use only features present in the source. Do not add generic landmarks to satisfy a category.

## Spot inks

Extract two to four spot inks from the photograph. Prefer desaturated colors such as carbon black, deep green, brick red, ochre yellow, slate blue, or taupe brown when supported by the source.

Do not force a fixed palette. Keep the photograph's distinctive color character. Use only a small area of color for emphasis.

The ink limit applies to the stamp, not the original photograph.

## Rubber stamp texture

Render each color as a separately hand-stamped layer. Include:

- Authentic carving texture and hand-engraved marks.
- Uneven line widths and contour notches.
- Fractured edges and dry ink shortages.
- Paper showing through granular ink.
- Uneven pressure and partial ghosting.
- Subtle misregistration that visually resembles about 1–2 mm at print scale.

Allow natural misalignment between color layers. Do not digitally smooth the edges.

The result must resemble a real carved stamp pressed onto aged paper. It must not resemble a filtered photograph, smooth vector illustration, or line-art logo.

## Field record typography

Use these elements when their factual content is known:

- English location name.
- `No.` followed by a number.
- Three short English keywords based on the scene.
- Gregorian calendar year.

Use a user-supplied number, or a simple sequence for the uploaded photos. Do not present that sequence as a historical record identifier.

Do not invent the location or capture year. Ask for missing facts when needed. If the user prefers no questions, omit uncertain fields. Never use the current year as an assumed capture year.

Place text below or beside the stamp in the whitespace. Use a small, restrained, slightly mechanically imperfect typewriter font.

Check spelling. Prefer adding text during compositing if generated lettering is inaccurate.

Make the typography feel like a traveler's field record, not an advertisement. Add no unrelated slogans, brands, or decorative copy.

## Visual character

Aim for field notes kept by an architect, travel writer, or natural observer: quiet, restrained, tactile, regionally specific, handmade, and collectible.

Trees, domes, and coastlines should remain recognizable without excessive description. The stamp should feel casually pressed into notes during a journey.

## Avoid

- Obvious central dividing lines.
- Circular seals, Chinese red stamps, postage perforations, and wax seals.
- Sticker collages and tourist souvenir templates.
- Smooth vector logos and generic city icons.
- Full replication of architecture or dense detail.
- Childlike craft styling, cartoons, and 3D rendering.
- Plastic textures, glossy digital gradients, and oversaturation.
- Excessive text and decorative clutter.
- Redrawing or altering the original left-side photograph.

## Final checks

- One output per photo; no collage or multi-image combination.
- Exact 4:3 landscape canvas; approximately 58/42 left/right split.
- No drawn dividing line.
- Original photo remains faithful, photographic, and undistorted.
- Warm, matte paper has subtle texture and generous whitespace.
- Stamp sits lower-middle and uses only 30–38% of the paper region's height.
- Distinctive source forms remain recognizable with minimal detail.
- Two to four separate-looking spot inks show handmade carving and printing defects.
- Text is small, accurate, restrained, and free of invented facts.
